Bifurcation diagrams

Author

Leonardo Capitani

Published

November 27, 2026

Four slides on how a bifurcation diagram is built from a Gaussian Process regression with time-delay embedding (GP-EDM), following Medeiros et al. (2025). Move between slides with the arrows; the first two are animated and have a video bar to play, pause and move back and forth.

Open the slides full screen

1. How a bifurcation diagram is built

Fix a driver, predict the next month, feed the prediction back as the new input many times, and plot the late values against the driver. Then change the driver and repeat. The equation on this slide is an illustration with invented numbers.

2. The Lake Zurich example

The same recipe with real data: large green algae and phosphate. The model is trained on January 1978 to October 1987; for 301 phosphate levels it is iterated 1000 months and the last 100 are plotted. It is a re-run of the authors’ data and code.

3. Algae, phosphate and the shift

The monthly series from 1978 to 2019, with the shift that Medeiros et al. identify in November 1987.

4. Uncertainty about the function

A Gaussian Process gives a best guess of the monthly prediction function, \(\mu\), and the uncertainty around it, \(S\). Panel a) iterates only \(\mu\). Panel b) iterates 500 functions drawn from the posterior \(f \sim GP(\mu, S)\) and shows how high the algae get across them. Here the model is trained on 1978 to 2019, so this describes the transition with its uncertainty and is not an early warning. The uncertainty covers the function only: the model’s settings are held fixed and no monthly noise is added. This panel is my own addition to the authors’ method.

Source

Medeiros LP, Sorenson DK, Johnson BJ, Palkovacs EP, Munch SB (2025). Revealing unseen dynamical regimes of ecosystems from population time-series data. PNAS 122, e2416637122. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2416637122. Data and code: https://github.com/lucaspdmedeiros/gpedm-regime-shifts (MIT License).
